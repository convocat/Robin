# Discovery material: Nest & Co Customer Care email handling

From Maaike's course (*GenAI design for conversations and content*, Session 4). Nest & Co is the
same fictional homeware shop Robin answers for — here the use case is the human email workflow,
not the chatbot.

## The request

> From: Ilse Verbeek, team lead Customer Care
> Subject: Help with our customer email
>
> Every November and December, our inbox runs over. We miss our promise to answer within two
> working days, and customers get different answers from different advisors.
> Can you help us find out where GenAI could help us, and where it shouldn't?

## Interview 1: Ilse Verbeek, team lead Customer Care

**Can you tell me a bit about your team?** Sure. I'm the team lead of Customer Care. I've been
here four years; I started as an advisor myself. We're six advisors now: four on level 1, two on
level 2. I'm level 3, so I handle the exceptions. We work weekdays, nine to five, by chat and
email.

**How much email do you get?** About 300 a day. On Mondays up to 450, because the weekend piles
up. And in November and December, it's about 50% more than usual. That's when it goes wrong.

**What kind of emails?** Mostly "where's my order", about 40%. Then returns, about a quarter.
Damage, maybe 15%. Product questions, 10%. The rest is complaints, changes, odd stuff.

**Can you walk me through how an advisor handles an email?** It's quite straightforward, really.
The email comes into our shared inbox. Advisors take the oldest one first. They read it, find the
order in the order system, and check the tracking with PostNL or bpost if it's about delivery.
Then they look up the rule in the handbook or the playbook. They decide what to do and write the
reply, using the templates in the playbook. Then they do whatever needs doing, like sending a
return label or planning a pickup. Finally they tag the email with a reason code and close it.

**How long does that take?** Six minutes on average. A "where's my order" takes two minutes. A
complaint can easily take fifteen.

**Who decides on compensation, like vouchers?** The advisors, within their level. Level 1 can
give up to 25 euros, level 2 up to 75. Above that, it comes to me. And in peak season, if an order
is more than five working days late, the customer gets a 15 euro voucher. That's standard.

**What goes wrong?** In November we never make our promise to answer within two working days.
Never. And advisors give different answers to the same question. A customer emails twice and
gets two different answers. That's embarrassing.

**If this works, what would be better?** Answering within one working day, also in peak season.
And the same answer, whoever replies.

**Is there anything you'd never want to automate?** Anything with money. Angry customers. And
anything about safety: if a bookcase falls over, I want a person on that the same day. And
honestly, at first I want an advisor to read every reply before it goes out.

## Interview 2: Kevin Smits, Customer Care advisor

**How long have you worked here?** Eight months. Before that I worked in a call centre for a
telecom company, so this is a lot calmer. Well, except now.

**Can you walk me through your morning?** I log in, and on a Monday there are usually about
eighty emails waiting. I'm supposed to take the oldest first, but honestly, I start with the
quick ones. The "where's my order" ones. You can do those in two minutes, the number goes down,
and you feel better. Then I do the hard ones.

**What do you need to answer an email?** The order system, the PostNL portal, and bpost for
Belgium. And the playbook. Not really the handbook, it's out of date. The return costs for
Belgium are wrong in there, for example. Everybody knows, but it's still in there.

**How do you write your replies?** I've got my own Word file with templates. I got it from a
colleague when I started, and I've changed it a bit. Everybody has their own version, I think.

**What about compensation?** That's the tricky one. I'm level 1, so I can give up to 25 euros.
But I never really know when. The playbook says only if the customer asks, but some customers
don't ask and they're really upset, you know? So I usually ask Ilse. She just knows. And the peak
season voucher is ten euros, that's in the handbook. At least, I think it's ten.

**Which emails take the longest?** Damage, definitely. You ask for photos, they send one photo,
you ask for one of the box, and they send it two days later. A damage case can take half an hour,
spread over a week. And then you have to decide: replacement, refund, a courier pickup for large
items.

**What's frustrating?** When customers ask two things and you only answer one. That happens when
it's busy. Then they email again, they're annoyed, and it's another email in the pile.

**Anything else?** German customers. We stopped delivering to Germany, but they still email
sometimes. I always send those to Ilse.
